How to clean vinyl records at home

Brush off the dust, wet the record, get the dirty fluid off, let it dry. Here’s how to do each step properly, with or without a vacuum.

By the Mikah team For vinyl LPs

The short version

To clean vinyl records at home, brush off the loose dust, spread a record cleaning solution over the grooves, then take the dirty fluid back off with a lint-free cloth or a vacuum. Rinse with distilled water if your fluid calls for it, and let the record dry fully before you play it.

Most records don’t need much. A bit of dust gets a quick brush. Fingerprints, residue, or a secondhand LP you just dug out of a bin need a proper wet clean. Cleaning takes care of dirt noise. It won’t fix scratches or worn grooves, so don’t scrub harder trying to chase out every pop.

This guide is for regular vinyl. Shellac 78s and lacquer discs are a different job and need different products.

What you need

Not a lot:

  • A soft dust brush for loose dust.
  • A record cleaning solution made for vinyl. Skip the household cleaners.
  • A velvet pad or a soft brush for the wet clean.
  • A lint-free cloth, or a vacuum record cleaner, to take the fluid back off.
  • Distilled water for rinsing or diluting, if your fluid says so.
  • A clean spot to lay the record, and somewhere to let it dry.

Keep the dry dust brush away from your wet tools. Look over the pad or cloth for grit before you start, since a grain of it dragged across the grooves can scratch the surface.

How to clean vinyl records by hand

01

Prepare a clean work area

Hold the record by the edges and the label. Lay it on a clean mat or a soft, clean surface. A dirty surface just moves its dirt onto the side you cleaned.

If you’re cleaning on the turntable, move the tonearm out of the way and keep the fluid well away from the electronics.

02

Remove loose dust

Brush the dust off before any liquid touches the record. Light pressure is enough. Wet dust turns to mud and gets into the grooves, so don’t skip this one.

Knock the dust off the brush before you pick up the next record. If a record is already in good shape, this may be all it needs before you play it.

03

Apply the record cleaning solution

Spray or spread the fluid over the playing surface and keep the label dry. A thin, even coat is plenty. Flooding the record only gives you more liquid to get back off.

Check the bottle: some fluids need diluting, and some need a minute to work. Don’t leave records sitting in it.

04

Work gently around the grooves

With the pad or brush, work around the record, following the grooves, with light pressure. There’s no magic number of turns. A record with a little dust needs less than one that spent ten years in a damp basement.

If the pad picks up visible grit, stop. Clean it or swap it before it touches the record again.

05

Remove the used cleaning fluid

This is the step that matters most. If the dirty fluid dries on the record, you’ve only moved the dirt around.

By hand, use a clean, lint-free cloth. Follow the grooves and switch to a fresh part of the cloth often. With a vacuum, suction pulls the fluid and the loosened dirt off the surface, which is what we built our brush for.

06

Rinse if your cleaning fluid requires it

Some fluids need a distilled water rinse and some don’t. If yours does, rinse, then take that water off the same way you removed the fluid.

Use a clean cloth or a clean pad for the rinse, so you don’t put cleaner back on the record.

07

Let the record dry completely

Make sure the first side is dry before you flip the record and do the other side. Let both sides dry fully before you play it or put it away.

Then use a clean inner sleeve. If the old one is dusty or damaged, replace it. Otherwise you’ve cleaned the record just to put it back in the dirt.

Cleaning vinyl records with a vacuum attachment

Hand cleaning and vacuum cleaning start the same way: fluid and a brush loosen the dirt. The difference is how the fluid comes off. You can use a dedicated record cleaning machine, or an attachment like ours on a vacuum you already own.

Hand cleaning

Brush the fluid in, then blot it off with a clean, lint-free cloth. It’s cheap, and it works.

Vacuum extraction

Brush the fluid in, then pull the dirty fluid off with suction, so there’s less left to dry on the record.

How often should you clean vinyl records?

Look at a record before you play it and brush off any dust. Do a full wet clean when you see fingerprints, dirt, or residue, when you’ve just bought a used record, or when it’s noisy and you think dirt is the cause.

How often depends on the record. One that gets handled a lot, like a DJ’s, needs it sooner than an LP that’s played a few times a year.

In between, hold records by the edges, keep your platter mat clean, and put each one back in its sleeve after playing.

Common questions

What is the best way to clean old vinyl records?+

Start with a gentle dusting, then decide if it needs a wet clean. Use a vinyl cleaning fluid and a soft brush, and get the fluid off carefully. Age doesn’t mean you need harsher chemicals or more scrubbing.

If a record is cracked, has visible mold, or might not be vinyl at all, stop and get advice before you clean it.

Can you clean vinyl records with a microfiber cloth?+

Yes, if it’s clean, soft, and lint-free. Check it for grit, press lightly, and move to a fresh part as you go.

A cloth that looks clean can still hold bits from last time, so wash it between sessions.

Can you clean vinyl records with water?+

Distilled water, yes, for diluting a fluid and for rinsing. Don’t use tap water, since it can leave minerals behind. On its own, water won’t do much for oily fingerprints.

Dish soap and household cleaners are a bad idea. Use something made for records.

Do you need a record cleaning machine?+

No. A brush, a cleaning fluid, and a careful hand method will clean your records. A machine, or a vacuum attachment like ours, is just another way to take the fluid off.

Why does a record still crackle after cleaning?+

Dirt is only one reason. Static, scratches, worn grooves, and a dirty stylus all cause noise too. Cleaning won’t repair damage, so don’t press harder trying to get rid of every pop. Clean your stylus while you’re at it.

When should you replace a velvet cleaning pad?+

When it’s worn or damaged, or when you can’t get the grit out. Look at it before each session.

On our brush the pad is self-adhesive. Peel off the old one and press a fresh one on, lined up so the opening stays clear.
